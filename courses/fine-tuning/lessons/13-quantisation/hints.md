`quantize`: `qmax = 2 ** (bits - 1) - 1`; `amax = w.abs().amax(dim=1, keepdim=True)` for rows or `w.abs().max().reshape(1, 1)` for the matrix; `scale = amax / qmax`; replace zeros with ones (`torch.where(scale == 0, torch.ones_like(scale), scale)`); `q = torch.round(w / scale).clamp(-qmax, qmax).to(torch.int8)`. `dequantize` is `q.float() * scale`. `quantization_error` is the Frobenius norm of the difference over the norm of `w`.
---
`QuantLinear.__init__`: copy `in_features`/`out_features`, call `quantize(linear.weight, ...)` and `register_buffer("qweight", q)`, `register_buffer("scale", s)`; with a bias `register_buffer("bias", linear.bias.detach().clone())`, without one set `self.bias = None`. `forward`: `y = x @ dequantize(self.qweight, self.scale).T`, plus the bias when there is one. `model_bytes`: `sum(t.numel() * t.element_size() for t in list(model.parameters()) + list(model.buffers()))`.
---
`quantize_model` is the walk of lessons 08 and 11: for each module and each of its `named_children()` (take lists, you modify the tree), when the child is an `nn.Linear` and its attribute name is in `names`, `setattr(mod, cname, QuantLinear(child, bits, per_row))`. The `LoRALinear`'s inner layer is called `base`, which is why `base` is in the default names. If the QLoRA test fails on trainable counts, check that you quantise *after* `add_lora` and that `QuantLinear` has no `nn.Parameter`.
